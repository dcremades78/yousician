01 · What we found

What we saw when we looked at Yousician on social.

Before writing anything, we looked at how people talk about Yousician and music-learning apps today. Four things stood out.

20M monthly users

A leading music app, but the creator layer is thin.

Yousician has over 20 million monthly active users across Yousician and GuitarTuna, with $50M in annual revenue. It teaches five instruments (guitar, piano, ukulele, bass, singing) with real-time feedback. Yet most of what shows up on TikTok is organic user content, not briefed creators speaking to local audiences.

People search for it

TikTok is a search engine for learning instruments.

Searches like "learn guitar app", "best app to learn piano" and "aprender guitarra desde cero" already have active TikTok discover pages. Videos made for those searches keep bringing trials long after they are posted. It's the approach we designed for Flo's US rollout.

Real-time feedback

The mic listens while you play. That is a hook.

Yousician uses your phone or laptop mic to hear what you play in real time, scoring every note and showing where you went wrong. That five-second moment on camera — play a chord, see it light up green — is the kind of hook that stops a scroll and makes someone start a trial.

Two untapped markets

UK and Spain are wide open.

Yousician already supports English and Spanish, with localised lesson paths in both markets. Music education content is booming on TikTok in both countries, but Yousician has almost no creator presence there. The cost per creator is a fraction of the US.

Sources: Yousician About page (20M+ users); Business of Apps on Yousician revenue; TikTok discover pages for "learn guitar app" and "best app to learn piano". Checked November 2026.

04 · A wildcard worth testing

Four creator types nobody expects from a music app.

Next to the shortlist, we'd keep a small part of the first wave for four creator types outside the usual music crowd. A few creators each, measured like the rest. If one works, it becomes a channel of its own.

Wildcard 1

Gamers who pick up a real instrument

Yousician looks like Guitar Hero but with a real guitar in your hands. Gamers already love rhythm games, and the jump from a plastic controller to actual strings is the kind of challenge they share. A side-by-side of the game screen and their hands is a hook that travels.

"Guitar Hero was the tutorial. This is the real thing."
#guitarhero · #gamer · #realguitar
Wildcard 2

University students learning a new skill

The "new hobby challenge" is one of the most popular formats on student TikTok. Learning piano in 30 days alongside lectures and deadlines is a story people follow. Yousician fits the productive-hobby trend without feeling like work.

"Learn piano in 30 days while surviving uni. Day 1."
#uni · #newhobby · #30daychallenge
Wildcard 3

Parents learning with their kids

A parent and a child sitting down to learn guitar together, following the same app on two devices. Wholesome content that families share. And it naturally shows the Family plan.

"My 8-year-old is already better than me."
#familytime · #learnwithme · #parenthood
Wildcard 4

Comedians who pretend they can play

An actor or comedian picks up a guitar and fakes a concert. Then they actually try to learn with the app. The before-and-after goes from comedy to genuine progress, and the transformation is the hook.

"I told everyone I play guitar. Now I have to actually learn."
#comedy · #fakeit · #beforeandafter
How we'd test it: two or three creators per wildcard inside the first wave, each with their own link, so you can compare cost per trial against the main shortlist. We'd scout these creators after our call.
